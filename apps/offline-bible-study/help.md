# Bible Study

A library of classic Bible study books, with the Bible open beside them.
Everything is built into the app — two Bibles, the fathers through the
nineteenth century, dictionaries, topical indexes, the Hebrew and Greek —
so nothing is downloaded and it works with no connection at all.

## The two halves of the screen

**The page** is the work you are reading: Matthew Henry's commentary,
the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, a dictionary, and so on. It is the
main thing on screen.

**The Bible panel** sits beside the page on a wide screen and below it
on a phone. The book-shaped button at the top shows or hides it.

Both halves are normally at the same place in the Bible. Turn a chapter
in one and the other turns with it. Tap a verse in the Bible panel and
the page moves to that verse; tap it again to open it for highlighting
and notes.

## Type size

Each half has its own. The small **A** and large **A** beside the tabs
size the work you are reading; the pair beside the translation's name
in the Bible panel sizes the Bible. They are separate on purpose — a
commentary is skimmed and scripture is read, and most people want them
set differently. Both are remembered.

## Choosing what to read

- **The library button** (top left, showing the current work's name)
  lists every work, grouped by age, oldest first. Beside each one is
  what it has for the chapter you are at — "7 passages", "112 topics",
  "nothing on this chapter" — so you can see who has something to say
  before you open it. Switch to **The whole library** to see what each
  work holds in all. Tap **About** beside any work for a plain
  introduction: who wrote it, when, what it is for, and how to read it.
- **The chapter button** (top, "John 1") picks a book and chapter.
- **‹ and ›** at the bottom turn chapters. On a keyboard, the arrow
  keys do.
- **Back and Forward** (top left) retrace jumps — a reference you
  tapped, a search result, a chapter you picked — like a browser.

## The three ways to hold a work

Above the page are tabs:

- **At John 1** — what the work has at the chapter the Bible is open to.
  This is the usual way to read.
- **Contents** — the work on its own, with no chapter in the way. A
  commentary opens as a book: pick a book, then a chapter, and ‹ › turn
  *its* pages while the Bible panel stays put. A dictionary, topical
  index or the gazetteer opens A to Z. Tap any reference to look at it
  in the Bible panel without losing your place in the work. The tab
  names where you are ("Volume · Romans 8", "A–Z · M") while you are
  reading this way; tap **At John 1** to come back.
- **About** — an introduction to the work, written for anyone.

Citations in the text are links. A Bible reference ("Gen 6:5", "John
viii. 22") opens the verse in the Bible panel. "See HENNA" in a
dictionary or encyclopaedia opens that article, landing on the section
when one is named ("See PRIEST, III"). "Chap. xi" inside a book opens
that chapter. A Strong's number opens the lexicon. The words themselves
are unchanged, so highlights and notes on the paragraph stay where they
were.

## What is in the library

Grouped by age, oldest first — the same order as the library button.

- **The original languages** — the Hebrew or Greek of the verse you are
  on, word by word, with a pronunciation, a short meaning, and a
  Strong's number that opens the dictionary entry.
- **Apostolic and Nicene** — Schaff's Ante-Nicene and Nicene fathers (37
  volumes), the Didache in Greek, Josephus in Whiston's English.
- **Medieval** — Aquinas's *Catena Aurea* (the Fathers on the Gospels)
  and *Summa*, the *Imitation of Christ*, the Latin *Glossa Ordinaria*,
  Palamas in Greek.
- **Reformation** — Geneva notes, Challoner's Douay notes, Calvin's
  commentaries and *Institutes*, Luther in the public-domain English we
  can keep, the Trent catechism.
- **Post-Reformation** — Matthew Henry (concise and complete), Lightfoot,
  Burkitt, Wesley, Poole, Owen, Edwards, the 1689 Confession, Gill's
  *Body of Divinity*.
- **Nineteenth century** — Abbott, the People's New Testament, the
  Fourfold Gospel, Spurgeon's Treasury of David, Barnes, Jamieson-
  Fausset-Brown, Clarke, Keil & Delitzsch, Scofield 1917, ISBE, Ryle,
  Haydock's Catholic commentary, Philaret's catechism, Hapgood's Orthodox
  service book.
- **Treasury of Scripture Knowledge** — for every verse, the other
  passages that speak of the same thing. Every reference is tappable.
- **Easton and Smith** — two dictionaries of Bible people, places and
  things.
- **Nave and Torrey** — two indexes of what the whole Bible says on a
  subject.
- **Gazetteer** — the places named in a chapter. Tap the coordinates to open that pin on Google Maps.
- **Your notes** — everything you have written, chapter by chapter.

A few works are in the language they were written (Latin, Greek, Hebrew)
because no public-domain English exists. They are in the library anyway. Each commentary opens at the chapter you
are in, or reads as a book from Contents.

## Search

The magnifier looks in everything at once. Under the box, tick or
untick where to look — Bible, the commentary you are reading, the other
commentaries, Dictionaries, Topics, Places, Hebrew & Greek, My notes —
and the ticks stack. When the Bible is ticked you can narrow it to the
Old Testament, the New Testament, or the book you are in. Results are
grouped by source, each with a count; the works answer first, the verse
list last.

**Just this work** (the Library row) confines the whole search to the
work you are reading — Henry alone, Easton alone, one Schaff volume —
without disturbing the ticks; **Everything ticked** hands them back.
Inside a commentary the Bible row still narrows it further, so "Just
Henry" on "the book you are in" reads Henry on John and nothing else.

A single word is matched exactly. A phrase — `human heart is bad` — is
also matched by meaning, under **Passages about this**, so a passage
can answer even when it uses different words.

Type a reference — `John 3:16`, `Ps 23`, `1 Cor 13:4-7` — to go
straight there, with what the library has at that place. Type a Strong's
number — `H430`, `G26` — to open that word.

## The Bible panel

- **The translation button** at the top of the panel switches between
  the World English Bible and the King James, or puts one beside the
  other in parallel columns.
- **Tap a verse** to move the page to it. Tap it again to open it for
  highlighting and notes. Tap the big chapter number to clear the
  selected verse and read the chapter whole.
- **Highlight** — select any words and a small colour menu appears. Tap
  a colour. You can rename the colours in Settings. The same menu works
  on the study material on the page.
- **Note** — write in the margin of a verse. Notes appear under **Your
  notes** in the library and in Search. **Voice note** records instead.
- **Compare** — the same verse in every translation, side by side.
- Small **\*** and **†** marks are the translators' own footnotes and
  cross references — tap them.

## Marking up the study material

Highlights and notes work on the work you are reading, not only on the
Bible.

- **Highlight** — select any words on the page and the same colour menu
  appears. It works across paragraphs: one drag, one gesture.
- **Note** — the menu also offers **Note**, which writes a note against
  the words you selected rather than against a verse. A passage with a
  note on it is underlined with a dotted line, whether or not you also
  gave it a colour; tap it to read or change the note.
- Tap any mark you have made to recolour it, take it off, or open its
  note.

These marks are anchored to the paragraph itself, so they are still
there when you reach the same passage from **Contents** instead of from
a chapter. They appear in **My highlights & notes** in Settings
alongside your Bible marks, and they are searchable, and they ride along
in **Back up my data**.

## Reading plans

The plan button at the bottom starts a reading plan and tracks your
days. Miss a day and the plan offers the next unread day.

## Read aloud

"Read aloud from here" on any verse reads the chapter to you, following
along verse by verse, using the voice set up in GifOS. Nothing leaves
your device.

## Reading together

Invite someone and you read the same page: when either of you turns a
chapter, the other's banner offers to follow. Your highlights and notes
stay yours — only the place is shared.

## Your data

Highlights, notes, voice notes and plans stay on this device, inside
the app. There is no account and no cloud. **Save** keeps them with
your copy of the app; send that copy to someone and they open your
Bible with your marks still in it.

**Back up my data** (in Reading settings) writes everything yours —
settings, highlights, notes, voice notes, reading plans — into one GIF
file stamped DATA BACKUP, wearing this app's own animation so you can
spot it in a folder. **Import** merges a backup in: records land by
their place, and nothing that only exists on this device is deleted.
The stamp is why you will never mistake the backup for the app itself.
