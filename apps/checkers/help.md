# Checkers

Jump the other side. Crown a king. Take every man, or leave them with no legal move.

This is a **10×10** board with **20** men a side, on the dark squares — the same board the original game shipped. It is not English 8×8 draughts, and it is not international draughts: men and kings both jump **one** man, **two** squares. Kings do not fly the long diagonal.

## Rules

- White sits the bottom and **goes first**.
- Men step one square **forward** on the diagonal.
- **You must jump** when a jump is on. A jumping man keeps going until it cannot. The piece pulses if you try a quiet step instead.
- Jumping is two squares over an enemy onto an empty dark square. The jumped man comes off.
- Reach the far row and the man becomes a **king** (a mark on the piece). A king may step or jump any diagonal, still one square at a time.
- Win by taking all of the other colour, or when they have nowhere to go.

## Controls

**Phone and mouse:** tap a piece, then tap the square it goes to. Legal landings light up; a capture landing looks different from a quiet step. Tap the same piece again (when you have a choice) to put it down.

There is no keyboard, no drag, and no zoom.

**Undo** walks back a full turn (your move, and the computer’s reply if it just played). **New game** returns to the setup card.

## Computer, two here, a friend

- **Computer** — you pick white or black. It thinks **on this device**. It is the original player: a legal move at random, a king preferred about half the time, and a jump when the rules demand it. It is not a strong engine.
- **Two here** — pass the device. White still goes first.
- **Play a friend**, then **Invite** in the bar above this app. White and black seats fill as people join; extras watch. Tap as usual on your turn. **Resign** gives the game to the other seat. After a short pause a new game starts. If the other player leaves mid-game, you win.

The board flips when you sit black, so your men still start at the bottom of the screen.

## What is saved

A local game in progress (computer or two-here) is remembered in this file — close it, come back, the same position is there. A finished local game is not kept as a history list. A friend’s board lives in the room, not in your private file.
