# Retirement Calculator

Answers two questions: **when can I stop working**, and **will the money last
once I do**. It opens already answering them, using the numbers filled in for
you, so you can start by correcting what is wrong rather than by filling in a
form.

## The two numbers at the top

- **Could retire at** — the earliest age at which your plan clears the bar you
  set (95% by default).
- **Could spend a year** — the largest steady budget that still clears it.

Both are solved, not guessed: the app runs your plan hundreds of times and
finds the edge.

Above them is the verdict — how many retirements your money survived out of all
the ones history offers, and when the worst of them ran dry.

## Everything is in today's money

Every figure you type and every figure you are shown is in **today's** dollars.
There is no inflation to add on and no future number to try to imagine: if it
says you could spend $72,000 a year, that means a life that costs $72,000 today.

## Filling it in

Six fields do most of the work — **your age, when you want to retire, how long
to plan for, what you have saved, what you add each year, and what you expect to
spend once retired.** Everything below them already has a sensible value and can
be left alone.

- **Other income** — a pension, an annuity, rent, part-time work. Tick *rises
  with inflation* for anything indexed. Social Security is; most company
  pensions are not, and over thirty years that difference is enormous. The app
  shrinks a non-indexed payment using real historical inflation.
  *Stops at age* means the last payment is the year **before** that age; leave
  it blank for life. And income that starts before you retire is added to what
  you **save**, not to what you live on — the app has no notion of what you
  spend while you are still working.
- **Big one-offs and college** — anything that lands on one year or runs for
  several: **college**, a wedding, a new roof, a few years of helping a parent,
  selling a house, an inheritance. Set *for how many years* to 4 and it bills
  every year for four years, which is one child in one row. **Add college**
  fills that in for you; change the number to yours. Money can point either
  way — there is a Paying out / Coming in switch, so you never type a minus
  sign.
- **Investments** — how much sits in shares rather than bonds, whether to drift
  towards bonds with age, and what you pay in yearly fees.
- **How you take the money** — a steady inflation-adjusted paycheck (the 4%
  rule), guardrails that trim after a bad year, a fixed percentage of the
  balance, a smoothed version of that, or spending it down deliberately.
- **How it is tested** — real history in the order it happened, or history
  reshuffled into a thousand new lifetimes out of real five-year blocks.

## The charts

- **What happens to the money** — every run at once. The shaded bands are the
  spread of outcomes, the blue line is the typical one, and the red line is the
  single worst stretch in the record. Hover, or use the arrow keys, to read any
  age. Every chart has a **See the numbers** panel with the same figures as text.
- **The price of spending more** — how often the plan works at every possible
  budget. Steep means a small change buys a lot of certainty; flat means it
  buys almost none.
- **Where each year's money comes from** — how much of the budget the portfolio
  has to find once pensions and other income have paid their share.
- **Rich, broke, or gone** — every year of retirement split by where you
  actually end up, including not being here. Running out is not the only way a
  plan ends, and for most people it is not the likeliest one. Survival odds come
  from the Social Security life table; the switch picks a man, a woman, or *at
  least one of a couple*, which is the one that matters for how long the money
  has to last.
- **What would change it** — the levers, each one re-measured against all of
  history. Press **Try it** to apply one and watch everything update.

## Light or dark

The sun/moon button at the top right switches between them, and it remembers.
It is your device's choice, not the plan's — if someone joins you through an
Invite they keep their own.

## Plans

Press **Save** to keep the plan under a name. **New** starts another,
**Compare** puts two side by side, and the name at the top left opens the list,
where you can rename, duplicate or delete.

Plans are saved on this device, inside this app. Nothing is uploaded, and there
is no account.

## What it does not do

- **No tax.** Add what you expect to pay to your yearly spending figure.
- **US market history.** Shares are the S&P 500, bonds are ten-year Treasuries,
  inflation is US CPI — Robert Shiller's monthly record, 1871 to now, which
  travels inside this app.
- **The past is not a promise.** It is the best evidence anybody has about how
  bad things can get, and it is still only what has happened so far.
