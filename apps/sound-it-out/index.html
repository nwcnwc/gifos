Your voice

Record yourself once, and your child hears you instead of the computer — for everything in the early levels, exactly as you said it.

The sounds are the part only you can do. An isolated “t” is exactly what a computer voice gets wrong, and a wrong sound teaches the wrong thing. Forty-two of them, about ten minutes.

The words and sentences are worth recording if you have the time — a real voice is always better — but until then the app uses its built-in voice, and if you set up a Text-to-speech model in GifOS Settings it can also read words you add later.

Each item is recorded here, one at a time, with the app checking as you go — it tells you straight away if something needs another go. GifOS does the actual recording and shows its own microphone indicator.

What's ready
    Your recordings

    Recordings are saved on this device, inside this app's icon. They never leave it: not to a server, not into the app you'd share. The one copy worth making is a backup — recording takes a while and cannot be redone exactly.

    The backup is a single GIF file with your recordings and word list inside it. Keep it on a memory stick or in your cloud drive; dropping it onto any GifOS Home Screen brings everything back.

    The voices, honestly

    Three tiers, in order: your recordings are used exactly as you said them, never altered. The built-in voice ships inside this app — each sound was prepared offline with the schwa (“tuh” for /t/) measured and removed, because a wrong sound teaches the wrong thing. And for words nobody recorded and nothing bundled — names you add later, pasted text — a Text-to-speech model from GifOS Settings reads whole words and sentences only. Isolated sounds are never sent to a model: that is exactly what they get wrong.
