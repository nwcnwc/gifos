Setup

Done once, shared by everything: the letter sounds, and the bank of words you have recorded.

  1. 1

    The sounds

    The part only you can do. The 42 letter sounds — sss, mmm, t. Sounding-out is built from these, and an isolated “t” is exactly what a computer voice gets wrong. About ten minutes. Until then, the app uses a real human starter voice for the sounds — never a computer guess.

  2. 2

    The word endings

    The magic-e endings — ase as in case, ike as in like, ome as in home — so words like “Chase” can build up as c + ase. Optional: the starter voice covers them until you do.

  3. 3

    Your word bank

    Every word you have recorded, shared by every sentence that uses it — so each sentence you add gets cheaper to record than the one before. Words get recorded where the sentences live, on the Sentences tab; this is where to listen back and redo any of them.

  4. 4

    Keeping it safe

    Recordings live on this device, inside this app's icon — not on a server, and never in the app anyone else would download. Recording takes a while and cannot be redone exactly, so the one copy worth making is a backup: a single GIF file with your recordings and your list inside it. Dropping it onto any GifOS Home Screen brings everything back.

The voices, honestly

Two voices exist, and nothing else: your recordings, used exactly as you said them — levelled to TV volume, never otherwise altered — and the starter voice, the app author's own recordings shipped with the app, used for anything you have not recorded yet. Your recording always wins. There is no computer voice anywhere: a word built up from sounds only makes sense when the sounds and the word are the same voice, so an entry nobody has recorded is left out of the video rather than faked.
