# Parable of the Polygons

A playable essay on how a tiny preference — “I wanna move if fewer than a third of my neighbors are like me” — can split a mixed town of triangles and squares. The words are the lesson; the boards are how you feel it.

## Read, then play

Scroll the post. Each dark well is a town. **Unhappy** shapes wiggle. You can pick those up and drop them on an empty square. Happy ones stay put.

On the bigger boards, **start** lets unhappy shapes hop to random empty spots on their own. The red number is how segregated the town has become. **new board** reshuffles.

## The sliders

- **I’ll move if less than X% are like me** — the bias. 33% is the essay’s default. Past that, the split gets sharp.
- On later boards, a second handle is **I’ll also move if more than Y% are like me** — a small demand for diversity. That is what mixes a segregated town back together.
- The last town also sets the **triangle:square** mix and how empty the board is. Those sliders are remembered the next time you open this file.

## With a friend

Whoever opens your link shares the **last town** at the bottom: the same board, the same sliders, the same running sim. Drag a shape, they see it. There is no in-app share button.

## Touch

Drag a wiggling shape; the rest of the page still scrolls. A shape that has settled will not pick up.

By Vi Hart and Nicky Case. Public domain. Based on Thomas Schelling’s 1971 model of segregation.
