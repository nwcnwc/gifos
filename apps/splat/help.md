# Splat

Open it and you are already standing in a small 3D scene — a ring of colour, a few glossy balls, a patch of ground. The scene is packed inside the app.

This is a viewer. There is no score and no opponent. Drag, pinch, or walk until the ring sits where you want it.

## Look around

**Mouse**

- Drag to orbit.
- Right-drag, or drag while holding Ctrl, to pan.
- Scroll to orbit a little. Shift-scroll pans. Ctrl-scroll (or pinch on a trackpad) moves closer or farther.

**Phone**

- One finger orbits.
- Two fingers pinch to move closer or farther, and slide to pan.

**Keyboard**

- Arrow keys walk. Hold Shift with up or down to rise or drop.
- `A` / `D` turn. `W` / `S` tilt. `Q` / `E` roll.
- `I` / `J` / `K` / `L` orbit.
- Space hops.
- `0`–`9` jump to a saved view. `-` and `=` cycle those views.
- `P` plays the slow spin.
- `V` puts the current view in the address bar.

The Keys panel in the corner is the original overlay. It stays.

## Your own scene

If you have a scene file (`.splat`, or a converted capture), drop it on the window to look at that instead. What you drop stays on this device for this session. It is not written into the app, and it is not sent anywhere.

This copy carries a tiny demo so it works with no network. A real capture you drop in will look much denser.

## Invite and Save

**Invite** in the bar above the app shares the app, not a dropped scene. A friend who opens the invite sees the packed demo, not whatever you dropped.

**Save** writes the app as it is. Camera position and a dropped file are not kept. Next open, you are back at the ring.

An unofficial port of [splat](https://github.com/antimatter15/splat) by antimatter15.
