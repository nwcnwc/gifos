# Tuner

Record a **note**, then see its **pitch**.

## Record a note

1. Press **Record a note**.
2. Play or sing one pitch — a guitar string, a whistle, a hummed tone.
3. Stop when the recorder finishes (a couple of seconds is plenty).

The app then names:

- The **note** (A, C♯, …) and octave
- The **frequency** in Hertz
- **Cents** sharp or flat of that note (0 is in tune; green within ±5 cents)

A wave of the take is drawn so you can see whether the clip was loud enough.

If it says **too quiet**, get closer or play louder. If it says it **heard sound but no clear pitch**, try a simpler tone — one string, a whistle, a hummed note. Strong extra frequencies can throw the reading off — that is the same limit the original detector has.

## Guitar, ukulele, bass

Pick an instrument to show its open strings. **Auto** names the nearest string. Lock a string and the needle is versus that pitch, even if another note is closer. Tap a string to **hear** it (a pitch pipe, not a recording).

**A4** (default 440 Hz) is the concert pitch the notes are named from. Lower or raise it if the room is not at 440.

## Not a live tuner

This is a take, then a reading. The needle does not move while you play. Record again for a new reading. There is no live microphone in here.

## Phone

A thumb on **Record a note** is the whole flow. String buttons and A4 − / + are thumb-sized.

## What is saved

The last reading — note, Hertz, cents — plus A4 and the instrument stay in this app file.

Unofficial port of [PitchDetect](https://github.com/cwilso/PitchDetect) by Chris Wilson.
